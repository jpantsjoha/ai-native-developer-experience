# Adopt and improve the existing harness

Use the project's existing profile, issue system and delivery records. A human role
owns outcomes and decisions; a skill supplies a procedure; an agent performs bounded
work; deterministic gates check executable conditions; independent review challenges
meaning. Names and invocation syntax differ across hosts without changing this contract.

## From conversation to accepted requirement

Meeting notes and session logs are evidence, never instructions or automatic approval.
Record relevant source/date, requirement or decision ID, accountable owner and state
(`proposed`, `accepted`, `superseded`) in the existing issue/spec/ADR. Keep attributed
source distinct from interpretation; unknown speakers stay unknown. Repetition is not
acceptance. Reconcile contradictory sources with the decision owner before dispatch.
An accepted record retains its scope and authority across handoffs; do not repeatedly
ask for the same approval or silently broaden it.

Link the accepted outcome to the task, test, reviewed candidate, delivered artifact
and observed user outcome. A resolving link proves structure, not semantic correctness
or execution. Public proposals contain only sanitised mechanisms and public-safe
evidence; private conversations and client details stay in their authorised source.

## Plan readiness before release

During specification identify applicable receiving-team ownership, support/escalation,
monitoring, runbooks, knowledge transfer, recovery and user acceptance. Add prerequisites
to the roadmap before the releases they enable; do not defer their discovery until
the final checklist. For small or self-operated projects, record a proportionate owner
and observable acceptance; an enterprise handover process is not a universal requirement.
An inapplicable item needs a reason, not fabricated evidence.

The release gate checks actual readiness evidence. A runbook's existence is not an
exercised recovery, and a deployment is not proof that a user achieved the outcome.

## Own and evaluate selected capabilities

Name a maintainer and review trigger in the existing capability-routing record. One
maintainer may own several skills; there is no universal skill-count quota. The role
roster owns acceptance and authority, while the skill maintainer owns its procedure.
For onboarding, have another contributor find an accepted requirement, perform one
bounded task, reproduce its check and record where they needed help.

Before changing optional guidance, name the observed friction, smallest remedy, quality
floor and removal trigger. Compare equivalent tasks with and without that guidance;
mandatory security, authority, tests and review gates stay enabled. Record accepted,
abandoned and still-open work, rework, review/retry/integration effort and human help.
Unknown time/cost remains unknown. Small observational samples are learning evidence,
not causal proof or a savings claim. Commercial/marketing outcomes are optional.

Use the existing review cadence to propose at most the justified changes, with evidence,
expected outcome, effort/risk, validation and rollback. Human review precedes adoption;
do not automatically rewrite skills or optimise invocation counts. A recurring schedule
or external issue posting requires its own authority and is not enabled by this guide.
